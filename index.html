Public writing

Op-eds & commentary

Utility Dive · April 2, 2026
California Approved a Gas Pipeline Solution. Now Comes the Hard Part. ↗

Approving SB 1221’s neighborhood-decarbonization pilots was the easy step; the hard part is picking zones and cost-effectiveness rules that counter utilities’ incentive to keep spending on gas pipelines. Argues the CPUC should use CalEnviroScreen to prioritize high-burden inland communities over well-organized coastal ones.

CalMatters · March 3, 2026
California Should Help Neighborhoods “Decarbonize,” Leave Natural Gas ↗

The state’s neighborhood-decarbonization pilots risk deepening energy inequity unless the CPUC targets the communities that need them most. Calls for transparent, data-driven zone selection over well-resourced coastal neighborhoods.

Memphis Flyer · December 11, 2024
Colossus ↗

xAI’s Colossus supercomputer runs on 18 gas turbines in South Memphis, emitting heavily without full air permits. The burden lands on a predominantly Black community with elevated asthma and cancer rates, for just 32 of a promised 300 jobs.

CalMatters · March 31, 2022
How We Might Better Live With Oil in California ↗

Examines California’s contradiction as a climate leader and a major oil producer, and proposes community-inclusive governance for oil and gas operations. Highlights the disproportionate health toll on Hispanic and Black communities living near wells. With Aaron Clark-Ginsberg.

RAND Commentary · March 2019
Lessening Leakages: How Water Systems Can Learn From Smart Electric Grids ↗

Applies smart-grid sensing and data analytics to reduce losses in water-distribution systems. An early, cross-sector case for sensor-driven infrastructure management. With M.E. Miro and M.E. Marlier.

The National Interest · October 2018
New Pakistan, Old Challenges ↗

Assesses Pakistan after Imran Khan’s PTI unseated entrenched dynasties in the 2018 election. Argues that persistent poverty and ambivalence on counterterrorism keep its democracy, and its nuclear arsenal, a global risk. With Rafiq Dossani.

SSRN Preprint · May 2026
The Causal Bar E3 Did Not Clear: A County-Level Re-Examination of Data Centers and Rates ↗

An independent, publicly-sourced re-examination of whether data centers shift costs onto ordinary ratepayers, drawing on 1,506 U.S. facilities and the full 77-tariff SEPA database. Host counties carry higher PM2.5, greenhouse gases, water withdrawals, and minority population share, and most large-load tariffs score poorly on customer protection. Preprint.
