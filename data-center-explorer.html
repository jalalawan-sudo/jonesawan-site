Does data center growth raise your electricity bill? It depends on the tariff it signs — weak terms shift stranded costs to you, strong terms make the data center pay its own way. ⓘ

Policymaker's must-dos for data centers

Five tariff elements determine whether the large load or the general ratepayer base bears the cost if demand forecasts are wrong ⓘ.

TermWhy it mattersStrong standard observed
Minimum billing demandSets the floor the data center pays even if it uses less — protects against under-recovery on reserved capacity.85% of contract capacity, ratcheted to the greater of that or 80% of the highest billing demand in the prior 11 months (Indiana Michigan Power)
Contract term & rampProtects against stranded assets if the load leaves early.12–15 years, ramp ≤4 years with stepwise floors (AEP Ohio, Dominion VA GS-5)
Exit feeDecides who pays for capacity built for a customer that then leaves.3 years of minimum charges plus net book value of incremental utility-funded facilities (AEP Ohio + Xcel MN combined standard)
CIAC (contributions in aid of construction)Decides who advances the capital for new infrastructure.Required upfront, customer funds planning and interconnection studies
Sales forecast matches the cost forecastRates are costs divided by sales. A utility that books new data-center infrastructure on the cost side has to book that same load on the sales side, or the rate moves in the wrong direction.Updated sales forecast filed with the cost ask, tested through intervenor discovery in the general rate case — see Utility Dive, Nov 2026 ↗
